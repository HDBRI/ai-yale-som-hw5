Question 1

How did the agents perform on each ticket, and why?

My grading rule: correctness first (were the numbers right, and did any fact get invented?), then safety (did money and promises stay where they should?), then efficiency (how much work and how many tokens to get there?).

B+

Ticket 101: Bulldog tee for Tauhid

The team found the real story: the tee isn't just out of stock. It's out of stock because the $840 rush reprint (invoice 501) is overdue, so Bulldog Print Co won't move. That is a three-table connection (tickets → inventory → invoices/vendors), and Inventory made it on its own with get_invoice(501).
What went right
  • Every number was right: S = 0 (short 1), M 5 / L 3 / XL 2, invoice $840, 3 days past due, 5-day lead.
  • Accounting looked at the whole cash picture, not just this bill: $3,400 − $2,400 rent − $840 = $160 < $500 reserve. It applied "rent first" and recommended hold, which is a real business judgment, not just arithmetic.
  • The reply to Tauhid didn't promise a date and didn't leak "we haven't paid our printer".
What cost it the A
  • Accounting sent work back to Inventory, so Inventory ran twice: 20 model requests and ~76k tokens for one $28 T-shirt.
  • Inventory raised a reprint purchase cheque with no amount. The cost ($8 unit cost in pricing) exists, but Inventory can't see the pricing tool, so I was asked to approve a blank cheque and declined it.
  • The customer reply said "CC-TEE-WHITE tee", a SKU code, where it should say "Classic Bulldog Tee".
  • "Resolved" here means "nothing waits on a human", not "Tauhid has a shirt". He still has to pick M/L/XL.

Question 3

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Honestly: all three tickets, as they're written, would have been simpler with one agent that has the same 14 MCP tools and the same approval gate.

  • 103 is the clearest case. One agent could do get_ticket → check_stock(M, 20) → get_sku_stock_all_sizes → get_sku_pricing → draft the reply: about 5 tool calls instead of 25, with nobody asking Inventory three times. Five agents added hand-offs and no new facts.
  • 101 is the same shape: stock → invoice → cash → reply. The one smart move (linking the missing tee to unpaid invoice 501) comes from reading tickets.invoice_id, which a single agent sees on the first call.
  • 102 is a 4-call job: get_ticket, get_rent_due(1), list_payments, prepare a $2,400 cheque. The Facilities → Accounting hand-off looked good, but one agent with both tools would reach the same verified $2,400.
  • The cost: 62 model requests and ~227k tokens for three tickets. A single agent would probably need about a third of that, and there would be no repeated reprint and invoice-501 cheques for me to decline twice.

Where the team still earns its keep, and why I'd keep it for a real shop:

  • Separation of duties for money: on 102, the agent that verified the landlord (Facilities) wasn't the one that prepared the payment (Accounting). A fake "rent due, pay this new account" email has to fool two roles, and then still needs my signature.
  • Least privilege: Customer Service, the agent that writes to outsiders, has no cash or invoice tools, so it can't leak "we owe our printer $840" even by accident. A single agent with every tool could.
  • Readable accountability: the board's desks and shift report say who decided what. That made it easy to spot that it was Customer Service, not Accounting, who invented "requested 25%".

My take: for read-heavy tickets like these, one agent plus the gate is simpler. The team is worth it for money flows and for many more ticket types, if we fix the fan-out with a rule like "use facts in your task; only delegate for something you can't look up yourself".

Question 5

Three new problems this team can't solve yet, and what we'd need to build

1
Getting paid: the AI Club hands over $348. Our tools only move cash down. record_payment pays bills, and there's no way to record money coming in, no orders table and no sales table. This is exactly why invoice 501 is still stuck: at $1,000 in checking, paying $840 breaks the $500 reserve (see the cash tab), and the club's $348 would make it $1,348 − $840 = $508, just enough. The team can't see that path.
Need: orders + sales tables; MCP tools create_order(customer, sku, size, qty, unit_price), record_customer_payment(order_id, amount, method) (cash up, also human-confirmed) and get_receivables(); and a Sales/Orders agent, with Accounting allowed to recommend "pay 501 once the AI Club money lands".
2
Actually ordering the 12 hoodies and the size-S tee reprint.
